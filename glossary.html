Home energy glossary

kW vs kWh, COP, SEER2, net billing, PPA — every term you'll meet in a solar, battery, heat pump or EV quote, in one sentence each.

ABCDEGHIKLMNOPRSTVW
AFUE
Annual Fuel Utilization Efficiency: the share of a furnace or boiler's fuel that becomes useful heat over a season. 95% AFUE means 5% is lost.
Air sealing
Closing gaps and cracks in a home's envelope to stop conditioned air leaking out. Often the cheapest energy upgrade.
Balance of system
Everything in a solar installation other than the panels: inverters, racking, wiring, disconnects and monitoring.
Battery round-trip efficiency
The share of energy put into a battery that comes back out, typically around 85–95% for home lithium batteries.
Blower door test
A diagnostic that depressurises a home with a fan to measure and locate air leaks.
BTU
British thermal unit: the heat needed to raise one pound of water by 1°F. 3,412 BTU equals 1 kWh.
Coefficient of performance (COP)
Heat delivered divided by electricity used. A COP of 3 means three units of heat per unit of electricity.
Cold-climate heat pump
A heat pump designed and tested to keep heating efficiently at very low outdoor temperatures.
Community solar
A shared solar farm that subscribers buy into or lease from, receiving bill credits without panels on their own roof.
Degradation rate
The yearly decline in a solar panel's output, commonly around 0.5% per year.
Demand charge
A fee based on your highest rate of power use (kW) in a billing period, common for businesses and some home tariffs.
Depth of discharge (DoD)
How much of a battery's capacity is actually used. Many home batteries allow close to 100%.
Ductless mini-split
A heat pump with indoor wall or ceiling units connected to an outdoor unit by refrigerant lines, with no ductwork.
Energy audit
An inspection of a home's energy use and losses, from a DIY walkthrough to a professional assessment with diagnostics.
ENERGY STAR®
A US government-backed label identifying products that meet energy-efficiency specifications.
Export rate / feed-in tariff
The price your utility or retailer pays for solar electricity you send to the grid.
Ground-source heat pump
A heat pump that exchanges heat with the ground through buried loops; also called geothermal.
Heat pump water heater
A water heater that moves heat from surrounding air into the tank, using far less electricity than a resistance tank.
HSPF2
Heating Seasonal Performance Factor (2023 test procedure): a US heating efficiency rating for heat pumps. Higher is better.
Induction cooking
Cooking with a magnetic field that heats the pan directly; fast, precise and efficient.
Interconnection
The utility approval process to connect a solar or battery system to the grid.
Inverter
Converts the direct current (DC) from panels or batteries to the alternating current (AC) your home uses.
kW (kilowatt)
A rate of power — how fast energy is used or produced at a moment.
kWh (kilowatt-hour)
A quantity of energy: 1 kW for 1 hour. The unit on most electricity bills.
Level 1 / Level 2 charging
EV charging from a standard outlet (Level 1) or a 240 V / 208 V dedicated circuit (Level 2), which is several times faster.
Microinverter
A small inverter mounted under each solar panel, letting each panel work independently.
Nameplate capacity
A system's rated maximum output under standard test conditions, e.g. an 8 kW solar array.
Net billing
A solar compensation scheme where exports are credited at a rate lower than the retail price.
Net metering
Exports offset imports one-for-one at the retail rate, usually reconciled monthly or annually.
Off-peak
Hours when electricity is cheaper on a time-of-use plan, often overnight.
Payback period
Years until cumulative savings equal the upfront cost of an upgrade.
Peak sun hours
Hours per day of sunlight equivalent to 1,000 W/m² — the key input for solar production estimates.
Phantom load
Electricity drawn by devices when switched off or idle; also called standby power.
Power purchase agreement (PPA)
A contract to buy electricity from solar panels a company owns on your roof, at a set price per kWh.
R-value
Resistance to heat flow for insulation. Higher R-values insulate better.
SEER2
Seasonal Energy Efficiency Ratio (2023 test procedure): a US cooling efficiency rating. Higher is better.
Self-consumption
The share of your solar output used in your home rather than exported.
Smart panel
An electrical panel that monitors and controls individual circuits, useful for battery backup and load management.
Solar lease
A contract where you pay a fixed monthly fee to use solar panels owned by a company.
Solar loan
Financing that lets you own a solar system with little or no upfront payment.
Standby power
See phantom load.
Thermal envelope
The boundary between conditioned indoor space and the outdoors: walls, roof, floor, windows and doors.
Time-of-use (TOU) rate
An electricity plan where prices vary by time of day, rewarding use in off-peak hours.
Virtual power plant (VPP)
A network of home batteries, EVs or smart devices coordinated to support the grid, often with payments to participants.
Watt (W)
The basic unit of power. 1,000 W = 1 kW.
