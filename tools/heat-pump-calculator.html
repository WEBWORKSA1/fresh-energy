How the heat pump comparison works

A furnace burns fuel to make heat; a heat pump moves heat from outdoor air (or the ground) into your home. Because moving heat takes less energy than making it, a heat pump delivers several units of heat for each unit of electricity — its coefficient of performance (COP).

The calculator first works out how much useful heat your home actually uses: fuel burned × the fuel's energy content × your current system's efficiency. It then asks how much electricity a heat pump needs to deliver that same heat at your climate's seasonal COP, and prices it at your electricity rate.

Worked example

700 therms of gas × 29.3 kWh × 85% efficiency ≈ 17,430 kWh of useful heat. A heat pump with a seasonal COP of 3.0 needs 17,430 ÷ 3.0 ≈ 5,810 kWh. At $0.17/kWh that's about $988 a year, versus 700 × $1.40 = $980 for gas — roughly a tie. Swap in heating oil at $3.80/gal, though, and the heat pump typically saves well over $1,000 a year.

The break-even electricity price

The single most useful number here. It's the electricity price at which a heat pump costs the same to run as your current system. If your rate (or your off-peak or solar-backed rate) is below it, the heat pump wins on running cost. Many utilities offer lower winter or heat-pump tariffs — check yours.

Picking a realistic COP

Cold-climate models keep working well below freezing, but efficiency drops as it gets colder. Seasonal averages of about 2.3–3.5 are typical, depending on climate, equipment and installation quality. Good sizing and duct design matter as much as the brand. See our heat pump guide.

Tip: replacing an air conditioner at the same time? Subtract the cost of a new AC from the heat pump price — a heat pump does both jobs.

Frequently asked questions

Is a heat pump cheaper to run than gas?

It depends on your gas and electricity prices and your climate. Use the break-even electricity price above: below it, the heat pump is cheaper to run.

Do heat pumps work in very cold weather?

Modern cold-climate heat pumps are designed to keep heating well below freezing, though efficiency falls as temperatures drop. Many homes in cold regions run on them year-round, sometimes with backup heat for the coldest days.

Where do I find my annual fuel use?

Your utility or fuel supplier statement usually lists annual usage. Subtract summer months (water heating and cooking only) to estimate the heating share.

What does AFUE mean?

Annual Fuel Utilization Efficiency — the share of fuel energy a furnace or boiler turns into heat for your home over a season. Older units are often 60–80%; modern condensing units 90–98%.
